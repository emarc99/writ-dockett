                                  13          Plaintiffs Newton AC/DC Fund, Patagon Management LLC, Scallion Trading Ltd., and

                                  14   Joshua Fong bring this class action complaint against defendant Maxim Ermilov. Plaintiffs allege

                                  15   that Ermilov breached a contract and fraudulently transferred over $15 million in cryptocurrency.

                                  16   They seek a constructive trust over the assets at issue. Plaintiffs now move for a preliminary

                                  17   injunction prohibiting Ermilov from transferring those assets pending resolution of this action. For

                                  18   the following reasons, the Court denies the motion.

                                  19                                             BACKGROUND

                                  20          Ermilov is the founder of Overnight, a company located and domiciled in the Cayman

                                  21   Islands. Overnight developed and launched the Overnight Protocol, a system of smart contracts.

                                  22   The protocol is governed by the agent of these smart contracts. Buying into the protocol is akin to

                                  23   buying into a money-market mutual fund: Any dividends generated by the protocol are deposited

                                  24   into certain cryptocurrency wallets, also referred to as the treasury.

                                  25          The protocol created and hosts USD+ (a stablecoin) along with the OVN token, which—

                                  26   among other benefits—grants holders the right to “govern” the protocol by changing the agent. On

                                  27   November 6, 2024, Ermilov posted on Discord that a holder of 51% of all OVN tokens could

                                  28   “vote to have POL [protocol-owned liquidity] distributed.” The named plaintiffs own

                                  14          Plaintiffs now seek a preliminary injunction prohibiting Ermilov from transferring the

                                  20                                           LEGAL STANDARD

                                  21          To obtain a preliminary injunction, a plaintiff must establish that (1) the plaintiff “is likely

                                  22   to succeed on the merits,” (2) the plaintiff “is likely to suffer irreparable harm in the absence of

                                  23   preliminary relief,” (3) “the balance of equities tips in [the plaintiff’s] favor,” and (4) “an

                                  24   injunction is in the public interest.” Winter v. Nat. Res. Def. Council, Inc., 555 U.S. 7, 20 (2008).

                                  25   “If a plaintiff can only show that there are ‘serious questions going to the merits’—a lesser

                                  26   showing than likelihood of success on the merits—then a preliminary injunction may still issue if

                                  27   the ‘balance of hardships tips sharply in the plaintiff’s favor, and the other two Winter factors are

                                  28   satisfied.’” All. for the Wild Rockies v. Peña, 865 F.3d 1211, 1217 (9th Cir. 2017) (quoting Shell

                                   2                                                ANALYSIS

                                   3          As an initial matter, this Court has authority to grant the requested remedy. In between

                                   4   filing their motion for a preliminary injunction and their reply to the opposition, plaintiffs

                                   5   submitted an amended complaint. The original complaint only asserted fraudulent transfer and

                                   6   breach of contract claims. But a court cannot issue a preliminary injunction pending adjudication

                                   7   of a contract claim for money damages. See Grupo Mexicano de Desarrollo S.A. v. All. Bond

                                   8   Fund, Inc., 527 U.S. 308, 333 (1999). The amended complaint adds a named plaintiff, removes

                                   9   any allegations concerning Circle (the entity that had the ability to freeze the funds at issue), and

                                  10   alleges that Overnight Finance is a shell corporation for Ermilov’s own affairs. It also adds two

                                  11   causes of action (constructive trust and fraudulent transfer under Cal. Civ. Code §§ 3439.04(a)(2),

                                  12   3439.05(a)), along with modifying its original fraudulent transfer and breach of contract claims.

                                  13          If a plaintiff amends her complaint after submitting her motion for a preliminary

                                  14   injunction, the amended complaint is the operative pleading. CDK Glob. LLC v. Brnovich, 16

                                  15   F.4th 1266, 1273 (9th Cir. 2021). Because the amended complaint includes a new claim for

                                  16   constructive trust, the Court has authority to issue the requested preliminary injunction. In re

                                  17   Focus Media Inc., 387 F.3d 1077, 1085 (9th Cir. 2004) (holding that Grupo Mexicano permits the

                                  18   issuance of preliminary injunctions freezing assets where cases include “fraudulent conveyances,

                                  19   and cases in which equitable relief is sought”). Plaintiffs, however, fail to establish a right to the

                                  20   preliminary relief they seek.

                                  21   I.     Class Certification
                                  22          Plaintiffs assert their claims on behalf of a proposed class of “all people and entities who

                                  23   currently hold OVN cryptocurrency tokens.” But “[w]ithout a properly certified class, a court

                                  24   cannot grant relief on a class-wide basis.” M.R. v. Dreyfus, 697 F.3d 706, 738 (9th Cir. 2012)

                                  25   (citation omitted). While a court may grant provisional class certification when considering a

                                  26   preliminary injunction, Meyer v. Portfolio Recovery Assocs., LLC, 707 F.3d 1036, 1041 (9th Cir.

                                  27   2012), such a grant cannot occur without an underlying motion providing evidence that the

                                  28   requirements for certification under Rule 23 are satisfied. Plaintiffs submitted no such motion

                                   3   (9th Cir. 1984).

                                   4   II.     Winter Factors
                                   5           A motion for a preliminary injunction is an evidence-based motion. Although the evidence

                                   6   standard is lower than for motions granting ultimate relief, the moving party must show—based on

                                   7   a preponderance of the evidence—that they are likely to succeed on the merits of their claims.

                                   8   Feldman v. Arizona Sec’y of State’s Off., 843 F.3d 366, 403 (9th Cir. 2016). No such showing has

                                   9   been made here.

                                  10           First, plaintiffs assert that Ermilov and Overnight are one and the same but provide no

                                  11   evidence to support their argument. They argue that his control over the wallets—as evidenced by

                                  12   the May 11 transfers—along with the “comingling” of the funds is enough to pierce the corporate
Northern District of California
 United States District Court




                                  13   veil between the company and its founder. They cite to a declaration by a partner in one of

                                  14   plaintiffs’ firms that describes the moving of the treasury assets, but no more. There is no

                                  15   explanation of the provenance of the assets in the wallet, nor anything that could help this Court

                                  16   conclude that Ermilov was not acting as an officer of the corporation Overnight.1

                                  17           Even if the Court were to conclude that plaintiffs have named the correct defendant, their

                                  18   claims are riddled with other issues. For example, their case is premised on Ermilov’s November

                                  19   6, 2024, post on Discord stating that a majority of OVN holders could vote to have the protocol

                                  20   owned liquidity distributed. Yet in their moving papers, they replace “protocol owned liquidity”

                                  21   with “treasury assets.” They offer no evidence sufficiently explaining why the two terms are

                                  22   interchangeable.
